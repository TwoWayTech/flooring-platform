# ADR 0025: Sales commission plans, advanced on confirmed payment and earned when the clawback window closes

Date: 2026-11-10. Status: accepted (the owner approved the Phase 0 plan on 2026-11-10) and built in feature batch 3.

## Context
Feature batch 3, Batch C1 moves commissions from Settings into HR: plans on a percentage of paid revenue or of gross margin, tiers by monthly target, a split between two reps, manager overrides, an optional draw, a clawback when an invoice is voided, refunded or written off within a set number of days, and monthly statements approved by HR or Owner, with adjustments that need a reason. The spec says commissions are "earned when the customer's payment is confirmed". Client note 5 asks for researched logic. Assumption C-2 hides commission from employees by default.

What exists today (ADR 0017, `packages/shared/src/commissions.ts`, `packages/shared/test/commissions.test.ts`):
- A rule is a basis, a rate and tiers, kept in `CompanySettings.commissions.rules`. Summit's only rule is 5 % of paid revenue, 6 % from $50,000. Nothing is stored per month: paid is simply earned minus this month's pending.
- `rateFor` applies the highest tier reached to the whole base of whatever range the screen asks for, so a year-to-date view is measured against a threshold meant for one month. The unit test fixes that behaviour.
- `teamStats` reads `Payment.date`, the day the customer paid, rather than the confirmation. Confirmation is `reviewedAt` on a reviewed payment; an auto-confirmed payment has `reviewedAt` null and its time is `recordedAt`.
- `paidShare` uses the whole payment, so an overpayment taken as customer credit (`validatePayment`) earns commission, while credit applied to an invoice (`InvoiceAdjustment`, kind `credit_applied`) earns none. Credit notes do not lower commission at all.
- **The margin basis on a part payment is arithmetically right.** The base is `payment × subtotal ÷ total × (subtotal − cost) ÷ subtotal`, so a quarter of the invoice earns on a quarter of the margin, with tax left out. Its weakness is rounding each payment on its own: across 480 trial combinations of invoice and payments, the sum missed the invoice's margin by at least a cent in 155 of them. The cumulative method below missed in none.

## What we learned

### Plan mechanics
- **When commission is paid.** Practice is a spectrum from paying at booking and clawing back only for non-payment, through staged payouts, to tying the larger part to cash collected when cash or revenue risk is high ([QuotaPath playbook](https://www.quotapath.com/resource/clawback-policy-playbook/)). In a survey of remodelers the commonest timing was half at sale and half at close (38 %), then when draws are paid (19 %) and at job completion (17 %) ([JLC](https://www.jlconline.com/business/sales-marketing/paying-to-win-sales/)).
- **Revenue or margin.** A flooring column put average associate pay near 8.9 % of sales on a margin just over 36 %, and suggested paying 25 % of profit, or 30 % on low-margin commercial and builder work ([FCNews 2016](https://www.fcnews.net/2016/07/dear-david-how-to-pay-millennial-sales-associates/)). Current retailers mix hourly pay, draws and sliding scales that rise with gross profit ([FCNews 2026](https://www.fcnews.net/2026/01/retail-compensation-evolves-with-the-times/)). Among remodelers that pay commission, 62 % tie it to per-job gross profit goals ([JLC](https://www.jlconline.com/business/sales-marketing/paying-to-win-sales/)). These are single, dated trade sources: useful as a guide, not as a benchmark.
- **Part payments.** ERP systems offer none, the whole amount on the first payment, or a proportional share of each payment ([Infor LN](https://docs.infor.com/ln/10.3/en-us/lnolh/help/tf/domains/cmg.disc.html)). A by-payment setting calculates on each full or part payment, on the paid amount ([MYOB Acumatica](https://help.myob.com.au/advanced/Docs/Published/UserGuide/Finance_Managing_Commissions_GeneralInfo.html)). We found no flooring source on how deposits specifically accrue, so treating a deposit as an ordinary part payment is our decision.
- **Tiers.** Whole-amount tiers apply the highest rate reached to the entire period; marginal tiers apply each rate only to the amount inside its band. On $220,000 with 4 % to $150,000 and 8 % above, that is $17,600 against $11,600 by our arithmetic ([Spiff on Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/salesforce-spiff-tier-payout-rules/get-started-with-tier-payouts)). Retroactive accelerators re-rate all revenue in the period once a threshold is hit ([QuotaPath](https://www.quotapath.com/blog/motivational-accelerator-plans/)).
- **Splits.** Split credit divides one sale and suits commission formulas; double credit suits bonus formulas and overpays if unchecked, so audit it when compensable revenue passes 112 % of actual ([WorldatWork](https://worldatwork.org/publications/workspan-daily/double-sales-crediting-when-and-why-to-apply-it)). Full credit can tip someone into an accelerator and raise cost even with the same payout split ([Bentega](https://www.bentega.io/blog/split-commissions-sales-crediting)). A split policy should say how thresholds and accelerators treat split credit, and whether a manager is paid on the credited portion or the whole deal ([Qobra](https://www.qobra.co/glossary/commission-split)).
- **Manager overrides.** Usually a percentage of the team's sales, paid to the manager on top of rep commissions, for example 2.5 % of $500,000 ([Everstage](https://www.everstage.com/resources/glossary/commission-override)). Others carve it from one pool, for example 8 % rep and 4 % manager out of 12 % ([Core Commissions](https://corecommissions.com/hierarchy-overrides-commissions/)). Sources disagree, so this is a design choice.
- **Draws.** A recoverable draw carries the shortfall forward: $1,500 paid against $500 earned leaves $1,000 owed ([Xactly](https://www.xactlycorp.com/node/1501)). A non-recoverable draw is a guaranteed minimum and nothing carries ([CaptivateIQ](https://www.captivateiq.com/blog/non-recoverable-draw)). One product instead pays a non-recoverable draw on top of commission, and offers a switch that forgives a negative balance ([Fullcast](https://support.fullcast.com/docs/draws)), so vendors use the words differently and our plan must define them.
- **Clawbacks.** Most policies run 60 to 120 days, 90 or fewer works well, and six months raised rep anxiety; most recover dollars rather than quota credit, net against future commission, carry any remainder forward, and some cap recovery at 25 to 50 % of variable pay per cycle ([QuotaPath playbook](https://www.quotapath.com/resource/clawback-policy-playbook/)). For one-time purchases 30 to 90 days is suggested, with recovery methods varying among customers observed ([CaptivateIQ](https://www.captivateiq.com/blog/navigating-clawbacks-best-practices)). The policy should be written into the plan and acknowledged by the rep ([QuotaPath](https://www.quotapath.com/blog/fair-clawback-policies/)).
- **Write-offs.** One ERP leaves a written-off balance out of the commission base: on a $1,000 invoice with $990 paid and $10 written off, the $10 earns nothing ([MYOB Acumatica](https://help.myob.com.au/advanced/Docs/Published/UserGuide/Finance_Managing_Commissions_GeneralInfo.html)).
- **Statements.** Tools add one or two management approvals with deadlines, keep an exportable history, and route a disputed figure back through recalculation and approval ([CaptivateIQ](https://captivateiq.com/blog/more-money-more-approvals)). We found no source describing how each product records the reason for a manual adjustment.

### California and federal rules
The official sites refused our tool (leginfo with 403, dir.ca.gov by connection). Statutes below are FindLaw's copies, stated as current to January 1, 2026; DLSE letters are a third-party copy that says it reproduces the official letter. Have counsel confirm against the official text.
- **Written plan.** An employment contract involving commissions must be in writing and state "the method by which the commissions shall be computed and paid"; the employer gives a signed copy and gets a signed receipt; an expired contract is presumed to continue until replaced. The definition leaves out short-term productivity bonuses and most bonus plans, but keeps an offer of a fixed percentage of sales or profits ([Labor Code 2751](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-2751/)), so a flat percentage of margin is covered. Commission wages are pay for selling the employer's goods or services, in proportion to the amount sold ([Labor Code 204.1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-204-1/)).
- **When it is earned.** A commission is earned once the employee has "perfected the right to payment" ([DLSE 1999-01-09](https://ezel.ai/labor-guidance/ca/dlse-1999-01-09)). It is due after the reasonable conditions of the agreement are met, and is not earned for Section 204 until the agreed calculation can be ascertained; a monthly program paid as soon as the rate is known appears to comply ([DLSE 2002-12-09](https://ezel.ai/labor-guidance/ca/dlse-2002-12-09-2)). Reasonable conditions may be put on vesting, but once vested a commission may not be forfeited, and holding earned commission for a quarter violates the twice-monthly rule ([DLSE 2003-04-30](https://ezel.ai/labor-guidance/ca/dlse-2003-04-30)).
- **Advances can be recovered when the plan says so.** An employer may advance commissions before all conditions are met and recover the excess from future advances; a 28-day hold was a condition precedent, and Section 221 reaches only wages already earned ([Steinhebel](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/ca-court-of-appeal/1445632.html)). Where reps agreed in writing that commission was not earned until the customer paid, chargebacks stood, and Section 224 was a second basis because the deductions were authorized in writing and did not touch base pay ([Koehl](https://hallapproved.com/ca/cases/appeals/2006/2296428)). A 365-day chargeback period survived, and the court distinguished a plan that had not called its payments advances ([DeLeon](https://hallapproved.com/ca/cases/appeals/2012/5811195)).
- **Limits on taking back earned wages.** An employer may not collect or receive back wages already paid ([Labor Code 221](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-221/)), subject to written-authorization exceptions ([Labor Code 224](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-224/)). Deductions for unidentified returns violate Section 221 by making salespeople "insurers of their employer's losses", while returns identified to that salesperson may be charged ([DLSE 1999-01-09, note 2](https://ezel.ai/labor-guidance/ca/dlse-1999-01-09), on *Hudgins*). Deductions must be tied to the employee's own sales, not general business costs ([Seyfarth, Cal-Peculiarities](https://www.content.seyfarth.com/publications/Cal-Peculiarities-2024/312)).
- **Draws.** A draw is recoverable only from commissions as they become due, not from final pay unless that pay is commission, and debts owed to the employer may not be taken from wages then due ([DLSE 1991-05-07](https://ezel.ai/labor-guidance/ca/dlse-1991-05-07)). The draw must cover at least minimum wage for all hours in the pay period ([DLSE 2002-12-09](https://ezel.ai/labor-guidance/ca/dlse-2002-12-09-2)). Recovering advances from future commissions is permissible ([DLSE 2002-06-13, cited by Seyfarth](https://www.content.seyfarth.com/publications/Cal-Peculiarities-2024/312)). An advance is not a wage, and deductions need written authorization and must leave at least minimum wage ([DLSE 2008-11-25](https://ezel.ai/labor-guidance/ca/dlse-2008-11-25-1)).
- **Termination.** Earned commissions are owed at termination and cannot wait for the usual calculation date ([DLSE 1999-01-09](https://ezel.ai/labor-guidance/ca/dlse-1999-01-09)).
- **Federal.** Wages must be paid free and clear, without kickbacks that cut into minimum wage or overtime ([29 CFR 531.35](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.35)), and employer losses such as customer non-payment cannot push pay below those floors ([DOL Fact Sheet 16](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/16-flsa-wage-deductions)). Commissions count in the regular rate ([29 CFR 778.117](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/778.117)); a commission computed over a longer period is spread back and adds half the rate increase for each overtime hour ([29 CFR 778.119](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/778.119)). Some retail staff are exempt under section 7(i) ([Fact Sheet 20](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/20-flsa-commissions-retail)).
- **Rest breaks in California.** A commission plan must separately pay for rest periods, and a draw did not cure it ([Ogletree on *Vaquero v. Stoneledge Furniture*](https://ogletree.com/shared-content/content/blog/2017/march/commissioned-california-employees-must-be-separately-compensated-for-rest-periods)). The statute on this names piece-rate pay only ([Labor Code 226.2](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-226-2/)), so the commission rule rests on the case.

**What this means for a plan that claws back after payment.** "Earned on confirmation, then clawed back" is the weak form: earned money that has been paid is what Section 221 protects. The form the courts upheld says in writing that the payment is an advance until a stated condition passes, recovers only from later commissions, and leaves base pay alone. So this design calls a confirmed-payment commission an advance until the window closes, and after that it is final. A clawback must point at an identified sale. Counsel must confirm this for each company's state before any real plan is signed.

**Not verified.** We could not open the Labor Commissioner's Enforcement Manual or any commissions FAQ on dir.ca.gov, the *Hudgins* opinion itself (read only through the DLSE letter and Seyfarth), or the *Stoneledge* opinion (read through Ogletree). A search result said employers should label advances as such on wage statements, but no page we could open said so; ask counsel. Only California and federal rules were researched.

## Decision

### Records
- **`CommissionPlan`** (new collection, replaces `CompanySettings.commissions.rules`; a change makes a new version, and a version used by a statement is never edited): `id`, `name`, `version`, `status` (draft, active, retired), `effectiveFrom`, `effectiveTo`, `basis` (paid_revenue, gross_margin), `baseRatePct`, `monthlyTarget`, `tierMode` (marginal, retroactive), `tiers` (`fromPctOfTarget`, `ratePct`), `overridePct`, `draw` (`kind`: none, recoverable, non_recoverable; `monthlyAmount`), `clawbackDays`.
- **`CommissionAssignment`**: `id`, `userId`, `planId`, `effectiveFrom`, `effectiveTo`, `monthlyTargetOverride`, `acknowledgement` (`signedName`, `signedAt`) or null. It records the signed receipt for Labor Code 2751.
- **`Invoice.commissionSplit`**: `[{ userId, pct }]`, at most two rows summing to 100, default the sales rep at 100; locked from the first confirmed payment.
- **`CommissionLine`** (computed while its statement is draft, stored once approved): `id`, `statementId`, `userId`, `kind` (earned, override, reversal), `invoiceId`, `sourceType` (payment, credit_applied, payment_void, credit_note), `sourceId`, `subjectUserId` (the rep, on an override), `planVersionId`, `collected`, `base`, `commission`, `confirmedOn`, `windowEndsOn`, `reversesLineId`, `reversalState` (applied, needs_review, waived). Whether a line is an advance or earned is derived from `windowEndsOn` and today (ADR 0013).
- **`CommissionStatement`** per person per month: `status` (draft, approved, paid), `carryIn`, `earnedTotal`, `overrideTotal`, `reversalTotal`, `adjustmentTotal`, `net`, `drawAmount`, `payable`, `carryOut`, `approvedBy`, `approvedAt`, `payPeriodId`. A statement can be approved only after its month ends and only when no line is `needs_review`, by HR or Owner, and never by its own rep (the same two-person idea as `reviewPayment`). Approved means frozen: corrections go on the next open statement. Paid is set when the pay period is exported.
- **`CommissionAdjustment`**: `id`, `statementId`, signed `amount`, `invoiceId`, `reason` (required, the `needReason` rule), `byName`, `at`, audited. A negative adjustment must name its invoice, because a deduction has to point at an identified sale.
- Permissions: `hr.commission.view` stays an alias of `sales.commission.view`; add `hr.commission.manage`, `hr.commission.approve` and `hr.commission.adjust`, for Owner and HR by default.

### Order of operations for one confirmed payment
`f(x)` is the plan's monthly curve, using `pctOf` with no rounding inside it. Marginal: each band's rate on the part of `x` inside that band. Retroactive: `x` times the rate of the highest band reached. Band edges are `target × fromPctOfTarget ÷ 100`. Each step below is one pure function in `packages/shared`.
1. **Gate.** The event is a payment now `confirmed`, or a `credit_applied` adjustment, on an invoice that is not void and not a credit note. `confirmedOn` is the date of `reviewedAt ?? recordedAt` (the adjustment's `at`), and it sets the statement month. The plan is the rep's assignment on that date; no assignment means no line.
2. **Net invoice.** `S`, `C`, `G` are the invoice's subtotal, cost and total, each less the same field on its non-void credit notes (`creditForInvoiceId`).
3. **Collected, capped.** `K = minDec(sum(confirmed payments and credit applied on the invoice), G)`. The cap stops an overpayment earning.
4. **Cumulative base.** `revenue = roundMoney(K × S ÷ G)`. Paid revenue: `base = revenue`. Gross margin: `base = maxDec(roundMoney(revenue × (S − C) ÷ S), 0)`. Zero `G` or `S` gives zero.
5. **Delta.** `delta = base − liveBase`, where `liveBase` is the invoice's earned lines less their reversals, for both reps. If `delta ≤ 0`, stop.
6. **Split.** First rep `roundMoney(pctOf(delta, pct))`; second rep the rest, so the shares sum to `delta`. Each rep's share is what counts toward their tier: whole-sale credit is left out because it pays on more than the sale ([WorldatWork](https://worldatwork.org/publications/workspan-daily/double-sales-crediting-when-and-why-to-apply-it)).
7. **Tier.** `before` is the rep's credited base this month in confirmation order (the step 1 timestamp, then id); `after = before + share`. `commission = roundMoney(f(after)) − roundMoney(f(before))`. Because the rounding sits on the running total, the month adds up to `roundMoney(f(month))` exactly, whatever the payment order.
8. **Override.** If the rep's plan has `overridePct` and the rep has `managerId`, the manager gets a line of `roundMoney(pctOf(after, overridePct)) − roundMoney(pctOf(before, overridePct))`: on the rep's credited base, on top of the rep's commission, flat, and not counted toward the manager's own tier. There is none on a manager's own sale.
9. **Store.** Every amount through `moneyString`; `windowEndsOn = addDays(confirmedOn, clawbackDays)`.

### A later void or credit note
- **Source line still on a draft statement:** the month recomputes as if the event never happened, so there is no reversal line.
- **Payment voided (bounced check, mistake), source statement approved:** reversal lines for exactly that payment's rep and override commissions, on the open statement of the event's month. Inside the window they apply at once. Outside the window they arrive as `needs_review`, and HR accepts or waives each with a reason. They are not automatic there, because the plan calls that commission earned; they are not ignored, because a void and re-record would otherwise pay twice.
- **Credit note (identified return):** re-run steps 2 to 4 with the new net. `R = liveBase − base`. If `R ≤ 0`, stop: a credit note never adds commission, and the next payment catches up through `delta`. Split `R` by the invoice's split, then walk each rep's live earned lines newest first. The first line with `windowEndsOn` before the event's date ends the walk, and the remainder stays with the rep, noted in the audit log only. Each chunk reverses `roundMoney(line.commission × chunk ÷ line.base)`, and a chunk that uses up a line takes all its remaining commission. The override line reverses in the same ratio.
- **Reversals never change a tier.** They carry dollars only, do not lower `before` in any month, and reopen nothing, in line with recovering dollars rather than credit ([QuotaPath playbook](https://www.quotapath.com/resource/clawback-policy-playbook/)).

### The statement and the draw
`net = earned + override + reversals + adjustments`. `A = net − carryIn`. `D` is the plan's monthly draw, or 0. `payable = maxDec(D, A)`. `carryOut` is `maxDec(payable − A, 0)` on a recoverable draw and `maxDec(−A, 0)` otherwise. `payable` is never negative, and it is the only figure the pay period reads, so a negative month cannot reduce base pay; it carries forward and is recovered only from later commission. On termination a final statement runs at once and `carryOut` is written off, not taken from final pay.

### Worked examples (checked against decimal.js with the repo's rounding)
**1. Margin plan, split, tier crossing.** Base 25 %, target $20,000, 30 % from 100 % of target, marginal, override 2 %. Invoice: subtotal $40,000.00, tax $3,300.00, total $43,300.00, cost $26,000.00, split Dana 60 % and Luis 40 %. Dana has $18,500.00 credited this month and Luis $3,000.00. A deposit of $21,650.00 (half) is confirmed.
- `K` is 21,650.00. Revenue is 21,650 × 40,000 ÷ 43,300 = 20,000.00. Base is 20,000 × 14,000 ÷ 40,000 = 7,000.00, half the invoice's margin of 14,000.00, as a part payment should earn. Shares: Dana 4,200.00, Luis 2,800.00.
- Dana goes from 18,500 to 22,700. `f` goes from 4,625.00 to 20,000 × 25 % + 2,700 × 30 % = 5,810.00, so her line is **$1,185.00**. Luis goes from 750.00 to 1,450.00, so his line is **$700.00**. Overrides: 454.00 − 370.00 = $84.00 and 116.00 − 60.00 = $56.00.
- Retroactive, Dana's line would be 6,810.00 − 4,625.00 = $2,185.00: $1,000.00 more for the same deposit. When the balance is later confirmed, base becomes 14,000.00 and `delta` 7,000.00.

**2. Clawback, inside and outside the window.** Paid revenue, 5 %, no tiers, 90 days. Invoice: subtotal $7,250.00, tax $643.44, total $7,893.44. Deposit $2,000.00 confirmed Sept 20: base 1,836.97, commission $91.85, window to Dec 19. Balance $5,893.44 confirmed Oct 20: base 7,250.00, delta 5,413.03, commission $270.65, window to Jan 18. Total $362.50, exactly 5 % of the subtotal.
- Jan 5, credit note for subtotal $1,450.00, tax $128.69, total $1,578.69. Net `S` 5,800.00, `G` 6,314.75, `K` capped at 6,314.75, base 5,800.00, `R` 1,450.00. The October line is still inside its window: 270.65 × 1,450 ÷ 5,413.03 = **−$72.50** on January's statement. Earlier statements do not change.
- Feb 1, credit note for subtotal $2,000.00, tax $177.50, total $2,177.50. `R` is 2,000.00, but the newest line's window ended Jan 18, so the walk stops at once: **nothing is reversed**, and $100.00 of commission stays with the rep.

**3. Void after approval, with a recoverable draw.** Dana's deposit check from example 1 bounces in December, after November was approved: reversals of −$1,185.00, −$700.00 for Luis, and −$140.00 for the manager, and November's tier base is untouched. Dana also earns $900.00 in December, so `net` is −285.00. With a $1,500.00 draw: `payable` 1,500.00, `carryOut` 1,785.00. January `net` 4,000.00: `A` 2,215.00, `payable` 2,215.00, `carryOut` 0.00.

## Where the research changes the spec
1. **"Earned when the customer's payment is confirmed."** With a clawback this is the weak form under Labor Code 221. Recommend wording it as: advanced when the payment is confirmed, earned when the window closes.
2. **No signed plan in the spec.** Labor Code 2751 requires a written plan, a signed copy and a signed receipt. Recommend a simulated typed-name acknowledgement on each assignment, as Batch D does for contracts, with a Home alert for HR when one is missing.
3. **"Invoice voided" cannot apply.** `voidInvoice` refuses once a payment is confirmed, so nothing has earned. Recommend replacing that trigger with "payment voided".
4. **"Refunded" has no invoice to point at.** `Refund` carries only a customer, and *Hudgins* needs an identified sale. A credit note has `creditForInvoiceId`, but the store has no action that creates one, although `voidInvoice`'s error tells users to issue one. Recommend the trigger be the credit note, and adding that action to Batch C.
5. **"Written off" has nothing to claw back on an on-collection plan.** Written-off dollars never earned, the demo caps write-offs ($5.00 at Summit), and reclaiming commission on money the company kept treats the rep as an insurer of its losses. Recommend removing it as a money trigger and only logging it.
6. **Gross pay.** A negative statement must not reduce the formula, so the pay period reads `payable`. For non-exempt staff with overtime, commission raises the regular rate, so the formula understates pay; recommend an extra line of half the rate increase for each overtime hour, or a clear warning. Also warn when a draw is someone's only pay, since rest breaks and minimum wage need separate cover.
7. **C-2 (hidden by default).** Keep the switch, as the client asked, but it should hide only the portal page. A rep on a plan still needs the signed plan and a statement, and California gives employees wage statements and access to payroll records ([Labor Code 226](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-226/)). Recommend default visible to anyone assigned to a plan, or, if hidden stays, HR told it must deliver statements another way.
8. **Margin visibility (CLAUDE.md rule 2).** A rep on a margin plan cannot check pay without the base, which is margin dollars. Recommend the owner approve a narrow exception: the base on a person's own lines, with cost and margin percent still needing `view_sensitive`.
9. **Batch D invoices.** Milestone and retainage invoices need their share of cost, or a margin plan pays on 100 % margin. Recommend Batch D set `Invoice.cost` proportionally.

## Consequences
- The real build runs the same pure functions on the server, inside the transaction that confirms or voids a payment or issues a credit note. Plans, assignments, lines and statements become company-database tables, and approved lines are immutable.
- `teamStats` must read commission from these lines, so there is one calculation. `rateFor` and the whole-base test are replaced, and seed figures change with marginal monthly tiers.
- The real build needs real e-signature for plans with the signed copy retained, payment of an approved statement on the first regular payday after month end (never quarterly), a per-pay-period draw, and the overtime true-up.
- Offboarding runs a final statement at termination, and nothing is deducted from final pay.
- Counsel must review before a real plan is signed: the advance wording, reversal of a voided payment outside the window, negative adjustments, write-off of `carryOut`, 7(i) status, how advances appear on wage statements, and the rules of every state outside California.
