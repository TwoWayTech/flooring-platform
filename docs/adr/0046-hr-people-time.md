# ADR 0046: HR part 1 on the API (people, timesheets with the clock, time off)

- **Status:** accepted, 2026-11-14
- **Context:** hr.md describes the demo's HR: people with onboarding and offboarding checklists and documents, weekly timesheets with the clock and overtime, time-off policies, a time-off ledger and requests, then pay periods, bonuses and contractor payments. The schema exists (`packages/db/src/company/hr.ts`, ADR 0024). HR runs in both products: Flooring Suite companies with the `hr` module and HR Suite companies (`PRODUCT_MODULES.hr` is `platform`, `hr`, `meetings`). Pay periods post accrual and gross pay, so they come in part 2.

## Decision
- **Module and product gating:** one Nest module, `modules/hr`, with `@RequireModule("hr")` on three controllers (People, Timesheets, Time off). Both products include `hr`, so one set of routes serves a Flooring Suite company with HR and an HR Suite company. Permission keys are exactly hr.md's "Who uses it" table.
- **Routes:** `GET /employees` (any of `hr.employee.view`, `hr.timesheet.view`, `hr.pto.view`, `settings.hr.manage`), `POST /employees` (`hr.employee.create`), `PUT /employees/:id`, `POST /employees/:id/checklist/:itemId`, `/offboarding`, `/documents` (`hr.employee.edit`); `GET /timesheets` (`hr.timesheet.view`), `GET /clock-entries` (`hr.timesheet.view`), `PUT /timesheets`, `POST /timesheets/:id/submit`, `POST /timesheets/upload` (`hr.timesheet.edit` for one's own, `hr.timesheet.approve` for anyone else), `POST /timesheets/decisions`, `POST /timesheets/:id/reopen` (`hr.timesheet.approve`); `POST /clock/in`, `/clock/out` (`hr.portal.view`); `GET /time-off-policies` (any of `hr.pto.view`, `hr.employee.view`, `settings.hr.manage`), `GET /time-off-entries`, `GET /pto-requests` (`hr.pto.view`), `POST /pto-requests`, `POST /pto-requests/:id/cancel` (`hr.pto.request` for one's own, `hr.pto.approve` otherwise), `POST /pto-requests/:id/decision`, `POST /time-off-adjustments` (`hr.pto.approve`), `POST /time-off-policies`, `PUT /time-off-policies/:id` (`settings.hr.manage`).
- **Feature switches:** People needs `hr.employees`, Timesheets `hr.timesheets`, the clock `hr.clock` (and the company's `clockEnabled`), Time off `hr.time-off`, policy writes `settings.hr`. `GET /employees` answers while any of the HR screens is on, and `GET /time-off-policies` while Time off or Settings → HR is on. Off, they answer 404.
- **Data scope** (hr.md `ownOrReports`, now `seesPersonRecords` in packages/shared): scope `all` sees everyone, `own` the person's own employee, `branch` adds direct reports (`Employee.managerId`). A person not linked to an employee sees nothing below `all`. Timesheets, requests and the ledger are filtered; clock punches are own-only below `all`. Writes about someone else check the same scope (404 outside it), decisions included, which the demo did not (hr.md edge case "Decisions do not re-check scope").
- **People list:** holders of `hr.employee.view` read every person, as the spec says. Everyone else reaches `GET /employees` only through timesheets, time off or settings, and gets the people their scope covers with contact details, birthday, emergency contact, documents and checklists left out except on their own record (hr.md edge case: "A real API must narrow this").
- **Pay rate** (`hr.employee.view_sensitive`, already `payRate` in `SENSITIVE_FIELDS`): stripped from every response without the key by the global interceptor. A save takes `payRate`, `payType` and `salaryHoursPerWeek` only from a holder; otherwise the stored values are kept (a new person starts at a `0.00` rate with the form's pay type and hours). Audit entries never carry the rate, only `payRateChanged` for holders. The body is strict Zod, so a Social Security, bank or any unknown field is refused and nothing like it is stored (CLAUDE.md rule 14).
- **Manager changes:** only someone with data scope `all` sets or changes `managerId`, on add or edit, because a manager decides `branch` scope; anyone else gets 400 (second security review).
- **Contact fields:** a save from someone without `hr.employee.view` keeps the stored email, phone and emergency contact (the birthday is not on the form), like the pay fields, and the answer shows them work facts only, as `GET /employees` does. No default role holds `hr.employee.edit` without `hr.employee.view`; a custom role can.
- **The employee link:** a sign-in is linked to an employee by `user_profiles.employee_id` (the existing column). `PUT/POST /employees` take an optional `userId` (a member of this company, not linked to anyone else, never the saver's own sign-in); null unlinks. The seed links Summit's users to their employees.
- **Separation of duties:** nobody decides or reopens their own timesheet, decides their own time off, adjusts their own balance, changes their own pay facts (rate, pay type, worker type, FLSA status, salaried hours), uploads their own hours without `hr.timesheet.edit`, or changes the link of their own sign-in. "Own" for these refusals is `selfCheck`: the employee linked to the sign-in, or any employee whose email equals the session's verified email (trimmed, case-insensitive), so an unlinked sign-in can't slip past them. The email match only ever refuses; data scope stays on the link alone. Because the email is what bars self-dealing for an unlinked sign-in, nobody changes the email on their own record, and nobody puts their own verified email on a record that is not already theirs (on add or edit), so the match can't be switched off and no second "self" record can be made (second security review). On their own record a person changes only the phone and the emergency contact (employee-portal.md rule 9): an allowlist, compared with the stored values after trimming, decimals by value and dates as strings, so an unchanged re-save passes and any other change (name, title, email, status, work state, hire date, manager, pay facts, commission visibility, any field added later) is 400 (third security review, which replaced the field-by-field checks). Nobody ticks their own checklist steps, starts their own offboarding or adds documents to their own file either. Deciders must be linked: timesheet decisions and reopens, time-off decisions, balance adjustments and pay-fact edits from a sign-in linked to no employee are refused unless it is the company Owner's ("Link your sign-in to your employee record before approving or changing pay; an Owner can link it."). This replaces the earlier choice not to require a link; the Owner is the exception because a sole Owner can't link their own sign-in (third security review). The self refusals still use `selfCheck`, link or email. Notices to approvers leave out a member whose sign-in email is the employee's, as well as a linked one.
- **Hours on the wire:** a timesheet's `days` travel as seven decimal strings (`"7.5"`, at most two decimals) both ways (CLAUDE.md rule 6), and the API stores and adds them with decimal.js (`hoursTextProblem`, `clockedHoursText`, `withClockedHoursText`). The shared `Timesheet` type keeps `days: number[]`, because the demo store, the screens and the shared overtime and flag rules read numbers and changing them all is a separate task; `TimesheetWire` is the API's shape, and the web client converts at the ApiClient boundary (`timesheetFromWire`, `hoursToWire`). The overtime split converts the stored strings to numbers only for `splitOvertime`.
- **Timesheets:** days are stored as `time_entries` rows (numeric hours). A save needs `needsTimesheet`, `hoursTextProblem`, a real workweek start (`workweekStartOf` with `settings.hr.workweekStartDay`) not in the future, and a sheet that is neither approved nor submitted. Decisions are all or none in one transaction; each approval's audit entry records the overtime split by `splitOvertime` with `overtimeRuleFor(work state, settings.localization.payroll.overtimeRule)`. Reopen, a save, every uploaded week and a clock-out that would write the sheet are refused while an approved or exported pay period overlaps the week (one shared query, `inClosedPayPeriod`); a refused clock-out keeps the punch open for HR to correct (third security review). Each approval's audit entry records the regular, overtime and double-time hours as decimal strings. The upload reuses the shared import plan (`IMPORT_HANDLERS.timesheets`) with only scoped people in reach, and saving re-plans under row locks. The shared resolver now refuses what a save refuses (someone who does not use timesheets, a future week, a submitted or approved week, hours outside 0 to 24 or with more than two decimals), and the API checks every written week again under the locks before it writes.
- **Clock:** both clock routes and `GET /clock-entries` need the `hr.timesheets` and `hr.clock` switches. Clock-in needs the company's `clockEnabled`, a linked employee who has not left (status not `terminated`) and uses timesheets, and no open punch. Clock-out adds the clocked hours to that week's draft or sent-back sheet; a submitted or approved one keeps its hours and only the punch is kept. A clock-out that would put more than 24 hours on the day is refused with a message to ask the approver to fix the week, so the punch stays open (a punch-correction screen is future work).
- **Time off:** `timeOffRequestProblem` (packages/shared, now also used by the demo) runs hr.md rule 11 in order. Approving re-runs `timeOffRequestProblem` under the employee lock, with that request left out of the pending hours, so a balance that dropped since the request (an adjustment, another approval) is caught; then it posts a `used` line, cancelling an approved request an `adjustment`; the ledger is append-only. Accrual posts with pay periods (part 2).
- **Settings:** the workweek, the clock and the overtime rule come from the company's `hr` and `localization` settings rows (CLAUDE.md rule 12). A company without them gets 409 `settings_missing` on hours and the clock, as sales does for tax.
- **Concurrency:** every write locks the employee row first, then the sheet or request, so two requests at once can't both spend a balance and two saves can't race a status. Writes that touch several people (batch decisions, the upload) lock the employees in employee-id order and then the sheets in id order (`lockTimesheets`), so two batches at once wait instead of deadlocking.
- **Notifications:** `timesheet.submitted` (in-app), `timesheet.decided`, `timeoff.requested` (email and in-app) go to the active members who may act on that employee: effective `hr.timesheet.approve` or `hr.pto.approve` by the shared engine (role ∩ company ceiling ∩ enabled modules, `AccessService.membersAccess`) and a data scope that covers the employee (`seesPersonRecords`), never the employee themselves; `timeoff.decided` and `timeoff.decided` (to the employee's linked, active user) through NotificationService after the commit; a failure never undoes the write.
- **Audit:** every write records hr.md's action strings in the same transaction. Saving hours, clocking in and clocking out are audited too (`Saved timesheet hours`, `Clocked in`, `Clocked out`), because CLAUDE.md rule 10 asks for every business write; the demo skipped them.
- **Documents:** employee documents use the proof-file path (ADR 0038), owner type `employee`, at most three per upload and no cap per person. `GET /files/:id` now asks the route for `platform` only, since HR Suite companies have no `core`, and checks each owner type's module in the service (`core` for payments, refunds, expenses, bills and supplier payments; `contracts` for contracts; `hr` with `hr.employee.view` and `hr.employees` for employee documents).
- **Web:** HR Home, People, Timesheets, Time off and Settings → HR read `hrData()` (people, timesheets, requests, policies, ledger, warehouses) from one source, and their writes are in `API_METHODS`.

## Consequences
- Pay periods, bonuses, contractor payments, commissions and the portal (`myPortal`, `updateMyEmployeeProfile`, and `clockIn`/`clockOut` from the portal) stay on the demo client until their tasks; in api mode the portal still reads demo records. The clock routes exist on the API for that task.
- Seeded accrual lines that an approved pay period posted are seeded without `pay_period_id`, because pay periods are seeded in part 2; their hours count in balances meanwhile.
- The template upload no longer has looser rules than the form (hr.md edge case "Template upload is looser than the form", closed by the first security review); the import wizard in the demo gets the same refusals.
- A new company can't file hours until Settings → HR and the payroll rule are saved (Settings moves to the API in its own task).
