# ADR 0024: HR timesheets, overtime by workweek on the true regular rate, time off, pay periods and the employee portal

Date: 2026-11-10. Status: accepted (the owner approved the Phase 0 plan on 2026-11-10) and built in feature batch 3.

## Context
Feature batch 3, Batch C asks for a full HR workspace (timesheets with approval and upload, time off with accrual and caps, pay periods with review and export) and a self-service portal, in both the Flooring Suite and the HR Suite. Assumption C-1 keeps the system to gross pay; taxes and net pay stay with the payroll provider. Assumption C-2 hides commission from employees by default. Commission plans belong to the commission research; this ADR takes approved commission amounts as an input.

Today `packages/shared/src/us-pack/overtime.ts` splits a week of daily hours, Monday first, under a federal or California rule. `packages/shared/src/types/hr.ts` has `Employee` (W-2 or 1099; hourly, salary per year or per job; no exempt status; one `ptoBalanceHours`), `Timesheet` (week start, seven daily totals; draft, submitted or approved) and `PtoRequest` (pending, approved or denied). `docs/FEATURES.md` lists `hr.dashboard`, `hr.employees`, `hr.timesheets` and `hr.time-off`.

How far the evidence goes. The federal regulations were opened through eCFR's developer API (issue date 2026-11-06), because eCFR's web pages turn away automated readers. `dir.ca.gov` refused connections and `leginfo.legislature.ca.gov` returned 403, so **every California rule below rests on excerpts shown in search results from those two sites, not on the full pages**. Gusto's help centre (403) and BambooHR's help centre (401) were closed in the same way. All of these are marked "(excerpt)". Nothing here is legal advice. Each rule needs confirming with the client's payroll provider or counsel before real money depends on it.

## What we learned

### Gusto
- **Approval.** Managers approve their direct reports' W-2 hours, with an approve-all action. Approval locks a timesheet until it is reversed, contractor hours are handled separately, and unapproved timesheets add a review step at the start of the payroll run ([review and approve hours](https://support.gusto.com/article/116295878100000/review-edit-and-approve-gusto-time-tracking-hours-for-managers), excerpt). I found no reject action; the documented route is to edit the hours or ask the employee to correct them. Payroll automations never approve a zero-hour timesheet on their own ([payroll automations](https://support.gusto.com/article/179037380821161/payroll-automations-for-admins), excerpt).
- **Bulk upload.** Hours come in as a CSV from the payroll run, with a downloadable template. A re-import replaces earlier entries, a zero overwrites a value and a blank leaves it alone, and an optional workweek column places pay in the right workweek for the regular rate ([Smart Import](https://support.gusto.com/payroll/payroll-settings/CSV-Upload/999913061/Upload-employees-hours-and-earnings-to-payroll.htm), excerpt).
- **Regular rate.** Commissions and non-discretionary bonuses count, and a bonus added after a payroll can recalculate the affected weeks and create an adjustment in a later payroll ([overtime](https://support.gusto.com/article/250220151117027/overtime-hours-and-pay), [regular rate](https://support.gusto.com/article/241120125852903/regular-rate-of-pay-rrp), excerpts).
- **Time off.** Accrual is by hours worked, a fixed amount, or unlimited, with per-period and overall maximums, a carryover limit, a yearly reset, waiting periods and optional negative balances ([time-off policies](https://support.gusto.com/article/999756591000000/set-up-and-manage-time-off-policies-for-admins), excerpt).

### BambooHR
- **Approval.** Each approver gets a summary with overtime and holiday hours; an approved timesheet locks and only a full admin can edit it; bulk approval runs from the payroll hours report ([approve a timesheet](https://help.bamboohr.com/hc/en-us/articles/360013162033-approve-a-timesheet), excerpt). I could not confirm a reject step.
- **Balances and accrual.** A balance is the sum of its events: accruals, manual adjustments, time used and carryovers ([time-off API](https://documentation.bamboohr.com/reference/time-off), excerpt). Accrual by hours worked waits for the timesheet to be approved ([approve a timesheet](https://help.bamboohr.com/hc/en-us/articles/360013162033-approve-a-timesheet), excerpt).
- **Who's Out and self-service.** The calendar shows who is away and upcoming time off, employees see their own leave beside the team's, and they can check balances and request time off ([time-off page](https://www.bamboohr.com/platform/time-and-attendance/time-off)). Views by week or month, filters and a setting for who may view it appear in the excerpts, and time-off visibility follows the access level, not the reporting line ([Employee Access Manual](https://help.bamboohr.com/hc/en-us/articles/227321928-Employee-Self-Service-Help-Manual), [time-off API](https://documentation.bamboohr.com/reference/time-off), excerpts).

### Rippling
- **Review.** Managers edit inline and approve in place, each entry keeps its change history, and missed clock-ins, overtime, job codes and break issues are flagged before payroll ([new Rippling Time](https://www.rippling.com/blog/introducing-the-new-rippling-time)). Timesheets can be reviewed inside each pay run, overtime and break policies follow the work location, and queries show who has not submitted ([product page](https://www.rippling.com/products/hr/time-and-attendance)).
- **Leave model.** Balances are read-only views of an accrual ledger. Requests move through pending, approved, rejected and cancelled, and are cancelled by status rather than deleted ([leave API guide](https://developer.rippling.com/documentation/rest-api/guides/leave)).
- **Not confirmed.** I could not confirm bulk approval, timesheet rejection or accrual settings from Rippling's own documentation.

### Federal law (FLSA and 29 CFR)
- **Weekly overtime.** Non-exempt employees are owed at least one and a half times the regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek, and a workweek is a fixed, recurring 168-hour period that may start on any day ([Fact Sheet 23](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/23-flsa-overtime-pay)).
- **Each workweek stands alone.** Hours cannot be averaged across weeks, however often the employee is paid ([29 CFR 778.104](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-778.104)).
- **Regular rate.** It is total pay for the workweek, less statutory exclusions, divided by hours actually worked, and it cannot fall below the applicable minimum wage ([Fact Sheet 56A](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/56a-regular-rate)). The overtime premium is half that rate on top of straight pay ([778.110](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-778.110), which works an hourly employee with a production bonus).
- **Commissions.** They are always part of the regular rate, however they are computed or paid ([778.117](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-778.117)). A weekly commission is added to that week's earnings ([778.118](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-778.118)).
- **Commissions and bonuses paid later.** When the amount is unknown on payday, overtime may first be paid without it. The amount is then spread back over the weeks it was earned and the extra half rate is paid for each overtime week ([778.119](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-778.119), [778.209](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-778.209)). If it cannot be tied to weeks, the regulation offers equal per week, or equal per hour when hours vary a lot ([778.120](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-778.120)).
- **Discretionary bonuses.** A bonus stays out only if the employer keeps discretion over both payment and amount until near the end of the period, with no earlier promise; the label does not decide it ([778.211](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-778.211)).
- **Timing.** Overtime earned in a workweek is due on the regular payday for the period in which that workweek ends, or as soon as practicable after if it cannot be worked out by then ([778.106](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-778.106)).
- **Paid leave.** Pay for vacation, holiday or illness is not pay for hours of employment and stays out of the regular rate ([778.218](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-778.218)). Overtime is owed on hours worked ([Fact Sheet 23](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/23-flsa-overtime-pay)), so paid-leave hours do not count toward the 40.
- **Salaried non-exempt.** The regular rate is the salary divided by the hours it is meant to cover. A monthly salary is multiplied by 12 and divided by 52; a semi-monthly salary by 24 and 52 ([778.113](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-778.113), [Field Operations Handbook 32b04a](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/field-operations-handbook/Chapter-32)). Two jobs at two rates give a weighted average ([778.115](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-778.115)).
- **Salaried exempt.** Deductions are allowed for full-day personal absences and, under a real sick-pay plan, full-day sickness; partial days are generally not deductible apart from unpaid FMLA leave and the first and last week, and the amount may be the daily or hourly equivalent of the weekly salary ([541.602](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-541.602), [Fact Sheet 17G](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/17g-overtime-salary)). A leave bank may be charged for a partial day as long as the full salary is still paid ([DOL advisor](https://webapps.dol.gov/elaws/whd/flsa/overtime/cr4.htm), [2005 opinion letter](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/legacy/files/2005_01_07_7_FLSA_PaidTimeOff.pdf), excerpts). The salary floor shown is $684 a week ([Fact Sheet 17G](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/17g-overtime-salary), revised 2019); I did not open a DOL page saying what became of the higher 2024 levels, so this needs confirming.
- **Sales staff.** Outside sales has no salary floor, but the employee must be customarily and regularly away from the employer's premises ([Fact Sheet 17F](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/17f-overtime-outside-sales)). The retail commission exemption needs a retail or service establishment, a regular rate above 1.5 times minimum wage, and more than half of earnings from commission ([Fact Sheet 20](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/20-flsa-commissions-retail)). Whether a flooring showroom qualifies is a question for counsel.
- **Contractors.** FLSA minimum wage and overtime apply only to employees, and neither a title nor a 1099 form settles status ([Fact Sheet 13](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/13-flsa-employment-relationship)). The DOL proposed a new test in February 2026; the page I opened still shows a proposal and says the DOL no longer applies the 2024 rule in its investigations, though that rule stays in effect for private litigation ([2026 rulemaking](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/flsa/misclassification/2026rulemaking), [Fact Sheet 13](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/13-flsa-employment-relationship)).
- **Unapproved work.** Management cannot accept work and not pay for it, and a rule against it is not enough ([785.13](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-785.13)).
- **Records.** Employers keep the day and time the workweek begins, daily and weekly hours, the regular rate, straight-time and overtime earnings and additions or deductions; payroll records for three years and time cards for two ([Fact Sheet 21](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/21-flsa-recordkeeping)).

### California (every item is an excerpt; confirm against the full text)
- **Daily overtime.** Hours over 8 in a workday, up to 12, and the first 8 hours on the seventh consecutive day of a workweek are paid at 1.5 times the regular rate. Hours over 12 in a workday and over 8 on the seventh day are paid at double ([DLSE overtime FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_overtime.htm), [Labor Code 510](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&division=2.&part=2.&chapter=1.), [AB 60 text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billCompareClient.xhtml?bill_id=199920000AB60)). A DLSE memo reads the seventh-day rule as needing work on all seven days of the workweek ([AB 60 update](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/ab60update.htm)); I could not confirm that view is current.
- **Workday and workweek.** A workday is a 24-hour period starting at the same time each day and a workweek is seven consecutive days starting on the same day each week. If the employer designates nothing, the DLSE assumes midnight and Sunday ([DLSE guidance](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/workdayandworkweek.pdf)).
- **Commissions.** One accepted method divides the week's total earnings by total hours, then adds one half of that rate for time-and-a-half hours and the full rate for double-time hours ([DLSE overtime FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_overtime.htm)). The FAQ speaks of piece and commission pay; I found nothing that deals directly with hourly pay plus commission.
- **Bonuses.** A non-discretionary production bonus is divided by total hours and carries the half-rate or full-rate premium. A flat-sum bonus is divided by the maximum legal regular hours in the bonus period and carries the full 1.5 or 2 times for each overtime hour ([DLSE overtime FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_overtime.htm)). I could not confirm how that divisor works in a short week.
- **Salaried staff.** A full-time non-exempt employee's regular rate is 1/40 of the weekly salary, and the salary pays only non-overtime hours ([Labor Code 515](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=515.)). The exempt salary floor is $70,304 a year in 2026 and rises to $72,384 in 2027 ([DIR 2025](https://www.dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2025/2025-118.html), [DIR 2026](https://www.dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2026/2026-66.html)).
- **Paydays.** Most wages are due twice a month, and overtime no later than the payday for the next regular payroll period; monthly pay is an exception for exempt salaried staff ([DLSE paydays FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_paydays.htm), [1993 letter](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/opinions/1993-04-19.pdf)).
- **Vacation.** Vested vacation cannot be forfeited, so use-it-or-lose-it is barred; a cap on further accrual is allowed; unused vacation is paid at the final rate when employment ends; a combined PTO plan is treated the same way; advanced vacation cannot be taken from the final paycheck ([DLSE vacation FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_vacation.htm), [Labor Code 227.3](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=227.3.)). DLSE letters give different figures, nine and seven months, for how long a worker must have to use vested time before a cap bites ([1993](https://dir.ca.gov/dlse/opinions/1993-08-18.pdf), [1998](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/opinions/1998-09-17.pdf)); I could not open them to say which is current.
- **Sick leave.** Paid sick leave accrues at one hour per 30 worked, use can be limited to 40 hours or five days a year, total accrual can be capped at 80 hours or ten days, and it is not paid out at separation but returns on rehire within a year ([DIR sick leave FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/paid_sick_leave.htm), [SB 616](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB616)).
- **What employees must get.** Wage statements show gross wages, total hours (not for salaried exempt staff), pay period dates and each hourly rate with its hours ([Labor Code 226](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=226.), [DLSE paydays FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_paydays.htm)). Commission terms must be in a written contract stating how commissions are computed and paid, with a signed copy and receipt ([Labor Code 2751](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=2751.)). An employer cannot require pay secrecy ([Labor Code 232](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?article=1.&chapter=1.&division=2.&lawCode=LAB&part=1.)).
- **Contractors.** A worker is presumed an employee unless the hiring company meets a three-part test ([Labor Code 2775](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=2775), [DLSE FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_independentcontractor.htm)). A construction subcontract can escape that test only with listed conditions, including a written subcontract and a state contractor licence ([Labor Code 2781](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=2781.)). I could not confirm how this applies to flooring installers, nor that Wage Order 16 ([text](https://www.dir.ca.gov/iwc/iwcarticle16.pdf)) is the order that covers them.

## Decision

### People and settings
- `Employee` gains `flsaStatus` (`non_exempt`, `exempt`, `not_applicable`; default `non_exempt` for W-2 and `not_applicable` for 1099), `exemptionBasis` (executive, administrative, professional, outside sales, or null), `salaryHoursPerWeek` (default 40, always 40 in California) and `commissionVisible` (null follows the company). The spec's W-2 hourly, W-2 salaried and 1099 types map onto the existing `workerType` and `payType`.
- An exempt profile below the salary floor gets a warning. Floors live in a table with effective dates (federal $684 a week; California $70,304 for 2026).
- **Settings → HR** adds `workweekStartDay` (default Monday, stated on screen because the law needs it designated), `payFrequency`, `payPeriodAnchor`, overtime rule by state, holidays, `showPayToEmployees`, `showCommissionToEmployees` (off, per C-2), `showRatesToEmployees` and `showTeamCalendarToEmployees` (off).

### Timesheets
- `Timesheet` gains `submittedAt`, `decidedBy`, `decidedAt`, `rejectionReason`, `source` (`form`, `upload`, `clock`) and `history[]` (who, when, action, reason). Status is `draft` → `submitted` → `approved` or `rejected`; a rejected sheet returns to the employee, who edits and resubmits.
- A rejection needs a reason and never changes the hours. An approved sheet is locked. HR may reopen it with a reason until the pay period is exported. Hours worked without pre-approval are still paid.
- The approval queue is per manager, with direct reports only, and rates stay behind `hr.employee.view_sensitive`. **Bulk approve** selects submitted sheets with no flags and shows the total hours and overtime before confirming. Flagged sheets need individual review: zero hours, seven days worked, a day over 16 hours (our own threshold), or hours on a day with approved full-day leave.
- Hours for 1099 workers are not collected through timesheets.

### Timesheet template and upload (CSV and Excel, one row per employee per day)
The upload reuses the ADR 0019 import wizard as a new `timesheets` kind, matching on email and then external ID.

| Column | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| `employee_email` | Required unless `employee_external_id` is given |
| `employee_external_id` | Optional match key |
| `employee_name` | Ignored; for the reader's convenience |
| `work_date` | Required; ISO or US date; must fall in a week not yet approved |
| `hours_worked` | 0 to 24, up to two decimals, not rounded; zero overwrites, blank leaves the value alone |
| `note` | Optional |

Refused rows: an unknown or inactive employee, a 1099 worker, a repeated employee and date, or a locked week. In the portal the same template comes pre-filled for one person and can write only that person's rows. Paid leave comes from the time-off module, never from this file, so it cannot be counted as hours worked.

### Overtime split
`splitOvertime` keeps its shape and adds the seventh-day rule. In California, when all seven days of the workweek have hours, the seventh day's first 8 hours are overtime and the rest are double time. The array starts on the company's workweek day. A timesheet day is a calendar day; a custom California workday window is not modelled.

### Gross pay per pay period (replaces the formula in C1)
For a non-exempt W-2 employee on base rate `b`, where `OT` and `DT` come from `splitOvertime`:
```
straight(P)  = sum over days d in P of hoursWorked(d) × b
paidLeave(P) = sum over days d in P of approvedPaidLeaveHours(d) × b
for each workweek w that ENDS in P:
  A(w)       = commission and non-discretionary bonus already allocated to w
  RR(w)      = (hoursWorked(w) × b + A(w)) ÷ hoursWorked(w)
  premium(w) = (OT(w) × 0.5 + DT(w) × 1.0) × RR(w)
trueUp(P)    = for each statement or bonus approved in P and each workweek w it covers:
               (OT(w) × 0.5 + DT(w) × 1.0) × (allocated(w) ÷ hoursWorked(w))
gross(P)     = straight(P) + paidLeave(P) + sum of premium(w) + commission(P) + bonus(P) + trueUp(P)
```
- An amount counted in `A(w)` is never counted again in `trueUp`. A week with no hours has no overtime, so it has no premium and no true-up.
- Unpaid leave is not subtracted for hourly staff, because they are paid only for hours worked. It is shown for information.
- **Salaried non-exempt:** `b = annual ÷ 52 ÷ salaryHoursPerWeek`, and `gross(P) = annual ÷ periods per year − unpaidHours(P) × b + sum over w ending in P of (OT(w) × 1.5 + DT(w) × 2) × b + commission(P) + bonus(P) + trueUp(P)`.
- **Salaried exempt:** `gross(P) = annual ÷ periods per year − unpaidFullDays(P) × (annual ÷ 52 ÷ 5) + commission(P) + bonus(P)`, with no overtime and no timesheet needed. A partial-day absence never reduces pay; it may draw on a leave bank. The form refuses partial-day unpaid leave for exempt staff.
- **1099 contractor:** not in the pay period at all. A separate **Contractor payments** list shows approved job or contract amounts, with no hours, overtime or paid leave, and its own CSV. A California 1099 worker with no contract or licence on file shows "Classification to confirm".
- **Allocation.** Each commission line goes to the workweek its customer payment was confirmed, matching the plan's "earned when confirmed" rule. An adjustment with no date is spread equally per hour across the statement month.
- `Bonus` is a new record: `employeeId`, `amount`, `kind` (`production` by default, `flat_sum`, `discretionary`), `periodStart`, `periodEnd`, `approvedBy`, `reason`. Discretionary bonuses stay out of `A(w)`. In California a flat-sum bonus uses `amount ÷ maximum legal regular hours × 1.5 or 2` per overtime hour.
- All rates use decimal.js and stay unrounded; money rounds to cents once per workweek premium and once per pay line. The on-screen label stays exactly as the spec gives it.

### Worked examples (rate $20 an hour unless stated)
1. **Hourly plus commission, federal.** 46 hours and $230 commission in the week. Straight pay 46 × $20 = $920; with commission $1,150; regular rate $1,150 ÷ 46 = $25; premium 6 × $12.50 = $75; **gross $1,225**. The spec's formula gives 40 × $20 + 6 × $30 + $230 = $1,210, which is $15 short.
2. **Same week, California.** Days 10, 8, 8, 13, 7 give 39 regular, 6 overtime and 1 double-time hour. Premium 6 × $12.50 + 1 × $25 = $100; **gross $1,250**. The spec's formula gives $1,230, which is $20 short.
3. **Semi-monthly period splitting a workweek.** Workweek Monday December 14 to Sunday December 20, 2026; periods December 1–15 and 16–31. Hours 10, 10 | 10, 10, 8 = 48, no commission. First period: 20 × $20 = $400. Second period: 28 × $20 = $560 plus premium 8 × $10 = $80, so $640. **Total $1,040.** Taken period by period neither half reaches 40 hours, so the spec's formula pays $960 and the $80 disappears.
4. **Monthly commission paid later.** A $880 statement with no usable dates, across 176 hours including 16 overtime hours. Rate increase $880 ÷ 176 = $5; true-up 16 × $2.50 = **$40**, paid in the period the statement is approved. The spec has no such line.
5. **1099 contractor.** Three jobs worth $3,000 in a 60-hour week. **Contractor payment $3,000**, no overtime and not in the W-2 file. The spec's formula would add overtime if hours were entered. If the worker were really an employee, the average rate of $50 would mean a premium of 20 × $25 = $500, which is the exposure the classification warning points to.
6. **Salaried exempt, semi-monthly.** $62,400 a year is $2,600 a period, $1,200 a week and $240 a day. One full unpaid day gives **$2,360**. A half-day absence gives **$2,600**, with no reduction.
7. **Seventh day, California.** Days 8, 8, 8, 8, 8, 8, 10 split into 40 regular, 16 overtime and 2 double-time hours, for a premium of $200. Today's `splitOvertime` returns 40, 18 and 0, for a premium of $180.

### Pay periods and export
- `PayPeriod`: `id`, `companyId`, `frequency`, `start`, `end`, `payDate`, status `open` → `in_review` → `approved` → `exported`. Approval needs every workweek ending in the period to be approved, or the employee excluded with a reason. Days of a straddling week whose sheet is not yet approved carry into the next period and are named on the review screen; example 3 assumes the sheet was approved in time. Approved can return to review before export. An exported period is locked, and later corrections become an adjustment line in the next period.
- `PayPeriodLine` holds the amounts below plus `weeks[]` (one per workweek ending in the period). A straddling week appears in the first period as "overtime settled next period".
- Monthly is refused when the company has non-exempt California employees.
- **Summary CSV** (one row per employee): `employee_external_id, employee_name, employee_email, flsa_status, work_state, pay_period_start, pay_period_end, pay_date, hours_worked, overtime_premium_hours, double_time_premium_hours, paid_leave_hours, unpaid_leave_hours, base_rate, salary_amount, salary_deduction, straight_time_pay, overtime_premium, paid_leave_pay, commission_pay, nondiscretionary_bonus, discretionary_bonus, overtime_true_up, adjustment, gross_pay_estimate`.
- **Workweek CSV** (one row per employee per workweek ending in the period): `employee_external_id, workweek_start, workweek_end, hours_worked, overtime_hours, double_time_hours, allocated_earnings, regular_rate, overtime_premium, straddles_period`.

### Time off
- `TimeOffPolicy`: `kind` (vacation, sick, personal, unpaid), `paid`, `accrualMethod` (`per_pay_period`, `per_hour_worked`, `annual_grant`, `none`), `accrualRate`, `capHours` (accrual pauses there), `annualUseLimitHours`, `carryoverHours`, `waitingPeriodDays`, `allowNegative` (false).
- `TimeOffEntry` is a ledger: `accrual`, `used`, `adjustment`, `carryover`, `forfeit`, `payout`. The balance is the sum of entries and replaces `ptoBalanceHours`. Hours-worked accrual posts when the pay period is approved.
- `PtoRequest` gains `policyId`, `decidedBy`, `decidedAt`, `decisionReason` (needed to decline) and a `cancelled` status.
- **California guards.** Vacation and personal policies refuse a `forfeit` entry and a carryover limit, and offer only a cap. The default cap is 1.5 times a year's accrual, which is our own conservative default and not a legal figure. Offboarding adds accrued vacation at the final rate, and never deducts advanced vacation. Sick defaults to one hour per 30 worked, a 40-hour or five-day use limit, an 80-hour or ten-day cap, and no payout.
- **Team calendar.** HR sees everyone with leave kinds; managers see direct reports with leave kinds. Employees see it only if the setting is on, and then only "Out", never the kind.

### Portal
- My timesheet, Clock in / out (optional per company; entries fill the draft), My time off, My pay, My commission, My documents, My meetings and My profile, for own data only. A hidden item is absent.
- 1099 workers get My documents, My profile and a list of their payments; no timesheet, time off or pay pages.
- When commission is hidden, My pay removes the commission line and its true-up and the total reads "Estimated gross pay from hours and leave", so the page still adds up and does not hint at what is hidden.

## Where the research changes the spec
1. **Overtime rate is wrong when commission or a non-discretionary bonus exists.** The spec multiplies by the base rate; both federal and California rules use the workweek's regular rate. Recommend the formula above (example 1).
2. **Double time has the same error** (example 2). Recommend the regular rate here too.
3. **Monthly commission statements need an overtime true-up.** The spec is silent. Recommend the `trueUp` line (example 4).
4. **A pay period is not a workweek.** Overtime is worked out by workweek and paid in the period where the week ends. Recommend the per-workweek calculation (example 3).
5. **"− unpaid leave" double-counts for hourly staff.** Recommend removing it for them.
6. **The salaried line misses exempt status, defines no daily rate and allows partial-day cuts.** Recommend `flsaStatus` and full-day-only deductions for exempt staff, and overtime for salaried non-exempt staff (example 6).
7. **1099 contractors do not belong in this formula.** Recommend the separate list, and the classification warning for California (example 5).
8. **Paid leave must not count as hours worked** for weekly or daily overtime. Recommend saying so in the rule, as the formula does.
9. **"Bonuses" needs a kind.** Recommend `production`, `flat_sum` and `discretionary`, defaulting to the included kind.
10. **The California seventh-day rule is missing** from the spec's wording and from `overtime.ts` (example 7), and Monday-first is hard-coded. Recommend adding the rule and `workweekStartDay`.
11. **Monthly pay periods do not work for California non-exempt staff.** Recommend refusing that setting for them.
12. **"Caps" need a California guard.** Recommend the guards above, since a yearly forfeit would be unlawful there.
13. **Reject must not alter hours, and approval cannot withhold pay** for unapproved work. Recommend treating rejection as a request to correct.
14. **C-2 can stay, with a note.** The switches hide portal pages only; they do not replace written commission contracts or wage statements. Recommend a note on the setting, a "signed copy given" date on the plan for California reps, and no pay-secrecy wording anywhere.
15. **"Employees see only their own data" conflicts with a shared calendar.** Recommend keeping it off for employees by default, with the setting above.

## Consequences
- The real API runs these same shared functions on the server and stores each workweek's inputs, regular rate, allocation and rule version, because the true-up and any audit depend on them.
- Salary floors, minimum wages and rates must be effective-dated. California's floor changes on January 1, 2027 ([DIR 2026](https://www.dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2026/2026-66.html), excerpt).
- Export layouts must be agreed with the client's payroll provider. Many may expect overtime hours at 1.5 times, whereas this file separates straight pay from the premium, so a mapping will be needed.
- Records must be retained for at least the federal periods ([Fact Sheet 21](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/21-flsa-recordkeeping)). California's own retention rules were not checked.
- Counsel must confirm the California rules against the full text before real payroll use. That covers the seventh-day reading, the flat-sum divisor, hourly-plus-commission, cap windows, installer classification and which wage order applies.
- Not modelled in the demo, and needed or to be ruled out in the real build: other states' overtime and leave laws (only federal and California were researched), California alternative workweeks, meal and rest premiums, two-rate weighted averages, the retail commission exemptions, fluctuating workweeks, FMLA and state family leave, local minimum wage and sick ordinances, and final-pay deadlines.
