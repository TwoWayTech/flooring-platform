# ADR 0026: Contracts, schedule-of-values billing and retainage on top of invoices and supplier bills

Date: 2026-11-10. Status: proposed (waiting for the owner's OK on the Phase 0 plan).

## Context
Batch D of feature batch 3 replaces Installation with Contracts, and assumption D-1 makes them two-directional: customer contracts (the business sells to builders, general contractors, property managers and dealers) and subcontractor contracts (the business hires crews and other trades). The spec says each milestone or schedule-of-values line can be invoiced, the invoice holds back retainage, retainage comes back on its own invoice at completion, and batch 2's invoices and payments carry the money. Today an invoice's balance is total minus confirmed payments, credits and write-offs (`ledger.ts`, ADR 0013), a bill's balance is its total minus confirmed allocations (`billLedger`), and "customers owe" is the sum of open invoice balances (`receivables.ts`). This ADR records what US practice and statutes expect, and the exact math, so that retained money neither overstates receivables nor goes missing. Nothing here is legal or tax advice: every state rule below needs the client's counsel and accountant before it is relied on.

## What we learned

### Pay applications and schedules of values
- The common US structure is a one-page summary with a line-by-line continuation sheet. The AIA instruction sheet for the continuation form shows, per line, a scheduled value, work from previous applications, work this period, materials presently stored, a to-date total, a percent, a balance to finish, and retainage where the rate varies by line ([G703 instructions](https://documentsondemand.aia.org/GeneratedFiles/G703-1992_Instr.pdf)).
- The summary page walks from the original contract sum, through net approved change orders, to the contract sum to date; it then takes the to-date total, subtracts retainage split between completed work and stored material, subtracts earlier certificates to reach the payment due, and ends with a balance to finish that includes retainage ([Knowify walkthrough](https://knowify.com/resources/aia-g702/)).
- Stored material is billed before installation. When it is installed it leaves the stored column and is counted in work this period, so the stored column is a balance rather than a running total ([AIA procedure, snippet only](https://content.aia.org/sites/default/files/2017-02/Payment%20Application%20Procedure.pdf)).

### Procore-style subcontracts and change orders
- A subcontract moves through statuses, and change orders and invoices become possible only once it is Approved or Complete. Line values are amount-based or unit-based, fixed before lines are added, and a default retainage percent is applied to schedule-of-values lines ([Create subcontracts](https://v2.support.procore.com/product-manuals/commitments-project/tutorials/create-subcontracts)).
- Retainage is set per line as dollars or a percent and released per line. A release may be partial and reduces the total retained on the line ([Set or release retainage](https://v2.support.procore.com/product-manuals/invoicing-project/tutorials/set-or-release-retainage-on-a-subcontractor-invoice), [Release retainage](https://en-ca.support.procore.com/products/online/user-guide/project-level/invoicing/tutorials/create-an-invoice-for-release-of-retainage)).
- Change orders run Draft, several Pending variants, Approved, Rejected, No Charge and Void; approved values count as approved changes and pending ones are reported separately ([Default statuses](https://support.procore.com/faq/what-are-the-default-statuses-for-change-orders-in-procore)). A third-party manual adds that only approved change orders reach the invoice and that a line cannot exceed 100 percent of its scheduled value ([Procore manual, third party](https://kbd.group/procore-manual/invoicing/)).
- California's SB 440 sets a claims process for change-order disputes on private works signed from January 1, 2026, with a 30-day review, undisputed sums due in 60 days and 2 percent monthly interest, until January 1, 2030 ([Gravel2Gavel summary](https://www.gravel2gavel.com/new-california-law-mandates-prompt-resolution-change-order-payment-disputes-private-works-improvement/)).

### Sales tax on retainage: there is no single rule
- Washington says tax is computed before retainage is deducted and the full selling price is reported without a retainage deduction ([Washington DOR](https://dor.wa.gov/education/industry-guides/construction/custom-construction)).
- A 1994 Texas Comptroller letter says tax on progress payments and retainage follows the taxpayer's books: due when recorded as a sale on accrual, due when cash arrives on a cash basis ([STAR 9408L1312D13, third-party copy](https://ezel.ai/tax-rulings/tx/9408l1312d13-repair-remodeling-renovations-contract-change-orders)). Deltek Vantagepoint offers both behaviours as a setting: tax when withheld, or tax when the retainage is invoiced ([Deltek](https://help.deltek.com/Product/Vantagepoint/4.5/bill_Taxes_on_Retainage.html)).
- Whether an invoice carries sales tax at all depends on the contract. Florida treats real-property contractors as consumers who pay tax to suppliers and charge none, unless the contract is an itemized retail sale plus installation ([Rule 12A-1.051](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-12A-1-051)). Pennsylvania has the contractor pay at purchase and not list tax as a separate charge ([61 Pa. Code 31.12](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/pennsylvania/61-Pa-Code-SS-31-12)). Colorado treats a lump-sum contractor as the consumer of building materials ([FYI Sales 6](https://tax.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/documents/FYI_Sales6.pdf)).
- New Jersey is the opposite for flooring: installing floor covering is a taxable charge even where it is a capital improvement, and a dealer working as a subcontractor collects tax from the general contractor ([ANJ-5, rev. 12/17](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/pdf/pubs/sales/anj5.pdf)).
- We could not verify when tax is due on retainage specifically in Colorado, Florida, Maryland, New Jersey or Pennsylvania. Texas rests on the 1994 letter only.

### Accounting for retained money
- The test is whether the right to payment depends only on the passage of time: if so it is a receivable; if it also depends on completing a milestone or further performance it belongs with contract assets and liabilities, assessed per contract ([Barnes Dennig on the FASB staff paper](https://www.barnesdennig.com/understanding-fasbs-guidance-on-retainage-for-construction-contractors/)). The FASB staff paper itself was not readable through our tools ([FASB paper, not read](https://storage.fasb.org/FASB%20Staff%20Educational%20Paper%E2%80%94Topic%20606%E2%80%94Presentation%20and%20Disclosure%20of%20Retainage%20for%20Construction%20Contractors.pdf)).

### Insurance certificates
- An ACORD 25 certificate is informational: it gives the holder no rights and does not change the policy, it lists each policy's number, effective and expiry dates and limits, and a certificate alone cannot make anyone an additional insured, which needs an endorsement on the policy; one guide suggests diarying the certificate 30 days before expiry ([how-to guide quoting the form](https://www1.wellesley.edu/sites/default/files/assets/certificates_how_to_read_and_review_with_acord_forms.pdf)). A business should collect the endorsement and compare it with the contract ([Miller Nash](https://www.millernash.com/industry-news/why-you-need-more-than-just-a-certificate-of-insurance)).
- When a certificate expires, a risk-management vendor's guide recommends a written hold on the work, written notice to the vendor, a check with the broker, verifying the actual policy, and keeping a record of the lapse before work resumes ([BCS, vendor guide](https://www.getbcs.com/blog/what-happens-when-a-vendors-insurance-expires-a-risk-managers-guide)). Whether a business may withhold payment depends on its signed contract ([Smart Compliance, snippet only](https://smartcompliance.co/?p=4957)).

### W-9 and 1099-NEC
- The December 2026 instructions set a $2,000 threshold for nonemployee compensation for tax years after 2025, possibly inflation-adjusted from 2027, due January 31 ([IRS instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec)). The change is statutory, applies to payments made after December 31, 2025, is indexed for calendar years after 2026, and also carries the backup-withholding threshold; the implementing regulations were proposed in spring 2026 and we did not confirm whether they are final ([IRB 2026-19](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-19_IRB), [Pub. 1099, snippet only](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p1099.pdf)). Backup withholding is 24 percent when no correct TIN is given ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/backup-withholding)). Payments to C and S corporations are generally excluded, and materials incidental to a service are reported with it ([IRS instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec)). Form W-9 gives the requester the payee's TIN and goes to the requester ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-9)).

### Lien waivers
- There are four types: conditional or unconditional, progress or final. A conditional waiver accompanies a request for payment and takes effect only when paid; an unconditional waiver states the money has been received, and California's form warns anyone not yet paid to use a conditional one ([CA 8132](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_8132), [8134](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_8134), [8136](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_8136), [8138](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_8138), [TX 53.284](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_53.284)).
- California voids a waiver not in substantially the statutory form and requires proof of payment for a conditional one, and it voids contract terms that waive these rights without a signed statutory waiver ([8132](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_8132), [8124](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_8124), [8122](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_8122)). Texas also requires substantial compliance with its four forms ([53.281](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_53.281), [53.284](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_53.284)).
- Florida offers forms it does not mandate, bars requiring a different form, and makes an advance waiver unenforceable; its progress form excludes retention ([713.20](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/713.20)). Levelset counts twelve states with statutory forms: Arizona, California, Florida (with that caveat), Georgia, Massachusetts, Michigan, Mississippi, Missouri, Nevada, Texas, Utah, Wyoming ([Levelset](https://www.levelset.com/blog/lien-waivers-12-states-with-required-forms/)). We confirmed the text for California, Texas and Florida only.

### Lifecycle and electronic signatures
- Contract-management vendors recommend storing renewal dates and notice periods as data and alerting at 90, 60 and 30 days before the notice deadline ([Sirion, snippet only](https://www.sirion.ai/library/contract-insights/prevent-missed-renewal-deadlines/), [ContractSafe, snippet only](https://www.contractsafe.com/blog/contract-renewal-best-practices)).
- Under ESIGN a signature or contract cannot be denied effect solely because it is electronic; consumers must consent before electronic records replace required written ones; and an electronic record satisfies a retention rule if it stays accurate and accessible ([15 U.S.C. 7001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001)). Texas's UETA lets a signature be attributed by any evidence, including an effective security procedure, and has the same retention test ([322.009](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._bus._and_com._code_section_322.009), [322.012](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._bus._and_com._code_section_322.012)).
- A credible audit trail, going by vendor documentation seen in snippets, lists the document or envelope ID, who sent it, each signer's name and email, each event with a timestamp, and often the IP address, which Adobe makes optional ([Docusign certificate, snippet only](https://support.docusign.com/s/document-item?language=en_US&bundleId=oeq1643226594604&topicId=gpa1578456339545.html&_LANG=de), [Adobe audit report, snippet only](https://helpx.adobe.com/sign/config/global/audit-report.html)). Neither statute prescribes fields, and a document hash did not appear in any source; it is our choice below.

## Decision

### Entities (`packages/shared/src/types/contracts.ts`)
- `Contract`: number (CTR-1001), direction `customer | subcontractor`, kind `master | project`, masterId, customerId or supplierId, parentContractId (a subcontract's customer contract, for pass-through reminders), title, scope, branchId, jobSite, startsAt, endsAt, renewal `{ mode: none | manual | auto, noticeDays }`, renewedFromId, originalValue (pre-tax, project only), retainagePct, storedRetainagePct, taxTreatment `on_billing | on_release | contractor_pays`, status, signedAt, substantialCompletionAt, finalAcceptanceAt, retainageDueBy, terminatedAt, terminationReason, files. A master has no value or lines; it carries pricing, terms and credit.
- `ContractLine` (schedule-of-values or milestone line): contractId, code, description, category (the tax engine's `TaxCategory`), billing `percent | milestone`, scheduledValue, retainagePct and storedRetainagePct overrides, milestoneDueAt, changeOrderId, estimatedCost.
- `ChangeOrder`: number, contractId, status, reason, lines `{ lineId | new line, description, category, amountDelta }`, submittedAt, approvedAt, approvedBy, signature, files. It may add lines or adjust a line's scheduled value.
- `ContractBilling` (pay application): contractId, number, periodEnd, status `draft | posted | void`, per-line inputs `{ lineId, completedToDate, storedBalance, retainagePct, storedRetainagePct }`, invoiceId or billId. Inputs are cumulative and rates are snapshotted per billing.
- `LienWaiver`: contractId, direction `given | received`, type (the four kinds), paymentId or supplierPaymentId, amount, throughDate, state, statutoryForm boolean, signedOn, files, status `requested | received | superseded`. Exchange rule: a conditional progress waiver goes with each request for payment and an unconditional progress waiver follows once that payment is confirmed; a conditional final waiver goes with the last request (including a retainage release) and an unconditional final waiver follows its confirmed payment. An unconditional waiver must reference a confirmed payment.
- `InsuranceCertificate`: supplierId, contractId, producer, holder, coverages `{ type: general | auto | umbrella | workers_comp, insurer, policyNumber, effectiveOn, expiresOn, limits }[]`, additionalInsuredEndorsementFiles, files. Status is derived from the earliest coverage expiry: valid, expiring (within 30 days) or expired.
- `SignatureRecord`: document id and version, SHA-256 of the PDF, signer name, email and role, consent wording version, recordedBy (the user who entered it in the demo), UTC time, and events (created, sent, viewed, signed, declined, voided). The UI labels it "Simulated signature" and keeps the signed PDF.
- `Supplier` gains kind `vendor | subcontractor` and `taxForm { w9OnFile, receivedAt, legalName, entityType, tinLast4, exemptFromReporting }`. The full TIN is never stored in the demo, as with card numbers. Cost-side amounts on subcontracts require `view_sensitive` (CLAUDE.md rule 2).

### Contract status (`Expired` is derived, like an expired quote in ADR 0012)
| Status | Meaning and guard | Allowed next |
|---|---|---|
| Draft | Editable. Leaving it requires line values to sum to originalValue (project) | Sent |
| Sent for signature | Lines locked | Active, or back to Draft (recall) |
| Active | Signed; change orders and billings allowed | Completed, Terminated |
| Completed | substantialCompletionAt set; the retainage clock starts; billing only of work already on the schedule | Closed, Terminated |
| Closed | Held retainage is 0, linked invoices and bills have 0 balance, final waiver on file or an Owner override with a reason | none |
| Terminated | Reason required; billing stops; held retainage stays until released or written off | none |
| Expired (derived) | Master or unsigned contract past endsAt. A project past endsAt stays Active with a "Past end date" flag, because work is unfinished | Renewal creates a new version |

Change order transitions: Draft → Pending → Approved or Rejected; Draft or Pending → Void. Approved is final and is corrected only by another change order. Only an Active or Completed contract accepts change orders and billings.

### Formulas (`packages/shared/src/contracts/billing.ts`, pure, using `money.ts` half-up rounding)
```
SV_L   = scheduled value of line L (original plus approved change-order deltas)
RCV    = originalValue + sum of approved change-order deltas (equals the sum of SV_L)
C, S   = cumulative work completed and materials stored balance of line L, at billing t or t-1; 0 <= C + S <= SV_L
G_L,t  = (C_t + S_t) - (C_t-1 + S_t-1)
Wr, Sr = work and stored retainage rates in force at billing t (line override, else contract)
R_L,t  = round2(Wr * C_t) - round2(Wr * C_t-1) + round2(Sr * S_t) - round2(Sr * S_t-1)
```
- Invoice for billing t: lines are one `DocLine` per schedule line with quantity 1, unit price G_L,t and the line's category; `subtotal = sum G_L,t`; `retainageWithheld = sum R_L,t`; `total = subtotal + tax − retainageWithheld + retainageReleased`.
- Tax uses the existing `computeTotals` per invoice. `on_billing` taxes G_L,t, so retained money is taxed now and its release carries no tax. `on_release` taxes G_L,t − R_L,t and taxes the release X_L later. `contractor_pays` shows no tax and needs a note, for the contractor-as-consumer cases above. Retainage is held on pre-tax amounts. Each invoice uses its own tax rule snapshot, so a contract's total tax may differ from a single calculation by cents.
- Release invoice: `subtotal = 0`, `lines = []`, `retainageReleased = X`, `total = X + releaseTax`. X is entered per line, or pro rata (X_L = round2(X × H_L ÷ H), the last line taking the remainder). X_L may not exceed the held H_L.
- Contract derived values: `billed G = sum G_L,t`; `withheld W = sum R`; `released X = sum X`; `held H = W − X`; `remaining B = RCV − G`. The contract page's "Invoiced" is G, "Retained" is H and "Remaining" is B.
- **Invariants (unit tests):** `G + B = RCV`; `0 <= C + S <= SV` per line; `0 <= H`; `(G − H) + H + B = RCV`; balance to finish including retainage `= RCV − (G − H) = B + H`; for constant rates, the sum of R per line equals `round2(Wr × C) + round2(Sr × S)` at the last billing; sum of invoice totals `= G + total tax`; paid plus credits, write-offs and open balances `= sum of invoice totals`.
- Only the latest posted billing may be voided, and only while no payment on it is confirmed. Over-billing is corrected by a lower cumulative on the next billing. The reconciliation test gains `total = sum of lines + tax − withheld + released`.

Change order effect: approval adds to RCV and updates lines immediately; issued invoices never change. A deduction may not take SV_L below C + S. Pending amounts appear as "Pending change orders" and are never billable. Approved change-order lines inherit the contract's retainage rates.

### Defaults
Retainage: 0 for masters; on a project, 10 percent, or the state limit from the table with a warning (not a block) when the user goes above it. Stored retainage equals work retainage. Tax treatment: `on_billing`. Alerts: insurance at 60, 30 and 7 days; renewal 90, 60 and 30 days before the notice deadline (endsAt minus noticeDays); milestones at 14 and 3 days. `retainageDueBy` is derived from the job-site state's deadline in the table and is shown as a reminder only. A release invoice's due date follows the customer's terms.

### Receivables: retained money is not yet due
- A progress invoice's `total` excludes retainage, so `invoiceLedger`, `receivablesSummary`, `customerReceivable`, aging and statements need no code change and "customers owe" stays correct. The same is true of "overdue".
- Do not reuse `dueAt: null` from ADR 0013 for this: it keeps the balance in receivables as current, which would overstate what customers owe.
- A new `retainageSummary` reports H per contract and customer for the contract page, the customer page and the Contracts dashboard widget. Statements and the aging report show it as a memo line that does not change the running balance.
- The release invoice is the point where the money enters receivables and starts aging, on its own issue and due dates. Before that it is a contract-level figure; past `retainageDueBy` it raises a reminder on the contract page, not an aging bucket.
- Reports that read `subtotal` (dashboard, analytics, commissions) see progress revenue at gross, and a release has `subtotal` 0 so nothing counts twice.

### Subcontract money through bills and supplier payments
- `Bill` gains optional `contractId`, `billingId`, `kind: progress | retainage_release`, `gross`, `retainageWithheld`, `retainageReleased`; a contract bill has `poId: null` and no receipts. `Bill.total` is gross + tax − withheld + released, with tax normally 0 unless the rule set requires it. `matchIssues` compares the bill's gross to the posted billing's gross rather than to a PO or receipts.
- `billLedger`, `allocate` and `SupplierPayment` stay as they are, including proof, approval and allocations over several bills. Held subcontractor retainage is a contract figure, separate from supplier balances; the Contracts widget shows "Retainage held" both ways.
- Example: a $18,500.00 labor subcontract at 10 percent, no tax. Progress bill 1: gross 7,400.00, withheld 740.00, `Bill.total` 6,660.00. Progress bill 2: gross 11,100.00, withheld 1,110.00, `Bill.total` 9,990.00. Held is 1,850.00 and is not in accounts payable. The release bill has `total` 1,850.00 and `retainageReleased` 1,850.00.
- A notification ties a retainage receipt on the parent customer contract to releasing the subcontractor's share, using the state's clock from the table. Lien waivers are requested with the bill and received against `SupplierPayment`.
- 1099 totals: confirmed allocations by payment date, per supplier and calendar year, whole amounts including incidental materials, excluding exempt entities, tested against a dated threshold table (2025: $600, 2026: $2,000, 2027 onward to be filled from the IRS). Retainage paid in January counts in January's year.

### Worked example 1: customer contract, tax on billing, stored materials, change order
Job in Colorado (example rate 8.81 percent on materials only), original value $120,000.00, below that state's $150,000 retainage threshold; L1 materials $72,000.00, L2 labor $48,000.00, 10 percent on work and stored. Change order CO-1 (+$15,000.00, new materials line L3) is approved after billing 2, taking RCV to $135,000.00.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Billing 1 | 0 / 30,000 | 6,000 | | 36,000.00 | 3,600.00 | 2,643.00 | 35,043.00 |
| Billing 2 | 42,000 / 0 | 24,000 | | 30,000.00 | 3,000.00 | 1,057.20 | 28,057.20 |
| Billing 3 | 72,000 / 0 | 48,000 | 15,000 | 69,000.00 | 6,900.00 | 3,964.50 | 66,064.50 |

## Where the research changes the spec
1. **Retainage percent has limits.** The spec treats it as free. Recommendation: the state table above, and a warning at contract entry (California and Colorado at 5 percent with their conditions).
2. **Release is not one event at completion.** Partial releases and state clocks exist, and Colorado allows a bond in lieu. Recommendation: allow partial release invoices, add `retainageDueBy` and a bond-in-lieu flag.
3. **Stored materials are missing.** Recommendation: add stored balances and a stored retainage rate, as in the formulas.
4. **Sales tax is not addressed.** Recommendation: the `taxTreatment` setting per contract, default `on_billing`, labelled "Example: confirm with your accountant", and New Jersey flooring treated as taxable labor.
5. **"Customers owe" is not addressed.** Recommendation: keep retainage off invoice totals until released, with memo lines on statements and aging.
6. **Supplier bills are defined as entered only against received goods.** That rule cannot apply to subcontracts. Recommendation: a contract path on `Bill` with no PO or receipts.
7. **The 1099 flag needs a threshold.** Recommendation: a dated table ($2,000 for 2026 payments, indexed from 2027) and a yearly total, plus a W-9 on-file field and a backup-withholding warning.
8. **Insurance is more than one expiry date.** Recommendation: one expiry per coverage with the earliest driving status, an endorsement file for additional-insured status, and a warning rather than a block on payment unless the company turns on a contract-based hold.
9. **Lien waivers need rules, not just records.** Recommendation: unconditional waivers tied to a confirmed payment, statutory-form flags per state, no blanket waivers before payment, and no generated legal forms in the demo.
10. **Completed needs a meaning.** Recommendation: define it as substantial completion, with a date that starts the retainage clock; Closed requires zero held retainage and final waivers.
11. **Expired needs a scope.** Recommendation: derive it for masters and unsigned contracts only; a project past its end date stays Active and flagged.
12. **One invoice per line is the wrong unit.** Recommendation: one invoice per billing period covering any number of lines, since retainage, tax and waivers attach to the pay application; a single line remains possible.
13. **Signature record.** "IP-free" is acceptable for the demo; the record should still hold a document hash, consent wording and event list, and be labelled simulated.

## Consequences
- The real build stores billings, retainage and change orders in tables with database checks for the invariants, and keeps cumulative inputs immutable once posted.
- General-ledger design must separate retainage receivable and contract assets per contract, with the unconditional test applied at each period end; this demo has no ledger.
- The state table becomes versioned data with effective dates, like tax rules, and needs counsel's sign-off; the SB 440 claim clock in California needs its own flow.
- Real signatures should move to a certified provider, with stored audit data, a tamper-evident document store, and IP capture where the client's privacy policy allows; ESIGN consumer consent applies if a homeowner signs.
- W-9 data with a full TIN must be encrypted and access-logged; the 1099 filing itself remains out of scope.
- Commission on payment is currently calculated from `subtotal ÷ total` of each invoice, so progress invoices pay commission on the full gross and a release pays none. The total is right but early by the retained amount; the real build should decide whether that is acceptable.
