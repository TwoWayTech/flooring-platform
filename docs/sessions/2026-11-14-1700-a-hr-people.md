Key decisions:
- **Pay rate:** stripped without `hr.employee.view_sensitive`, kept on saves by others, never changed by its owner, never in audit.
- **Data scope** (`ownOrReports`): own, or own plus direct reports, or all.
- **Separation of duties:** nobody decides their own timesheet or time off.
- **Validation:** strict Zod refuses any Social Security or bank field.
- **Files:** `GET /files/:id` asks the route for the `platform` module, and each file type checks its own module.

## Reviews
First security review: 2 blocking (submitted-week upload; self-guards failing open for unlinked sign-ins), fixed; should-fix items 3–9 done.
1. Upload over a submitted week: the shared timesheet resolver refuses submitted weeks, future weeks, people who don't use timesheets and bad hours; the API checks each written week again under the locks.
2. Self-guards for unlinked sign-ins: `selfCheck` treats an employee as one's own by the sign-in link or the verified email, for refusals only (pay, decisions, reopen, balance adjustments, own-hours edits).
3. Own pay facts: nobody changes their own pay type, worker type, FLSA status or salaried hours either.
4. Notifications: approvers are chosen by effective permissions (`AccessService.membersAccess`) and a data scope that covers the employee.
5. Deadlock: batch decisions lock employees in id order, then sheets (`lockTimesheets`); the upload locks sheets in id order after the employees.
6. Clock-out: refused when the day would pass 24 hours.
7. Without view_sensitive the stored pay type and salaried hours stay, like the rate.
8. Hours as decimal strings on the API (`TimesheetWire`); the web client converts at the ApiClient boundary (ADR 0046).
9. An approver's upload refuses their own rows without `hr.timesheet.edit`, and nobody reopens their own week.
